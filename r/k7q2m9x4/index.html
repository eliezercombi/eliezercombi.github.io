1. Fix these before spending on ads

These are not about wording. Any one of them can stop a campaign on its first day, so they come before the listing.

The store says 12+, the app says 19+.

Google Play shows the rating "12+, parental guidance recommended". The description says the app is for people aged 19 and over. For an app where strangers video-call each other, in the Dating category, that contradiction is a common reason for a rejected update, a removed listing and disapproved ads. Redo the content rating questionnaire in Play Console (Policy, App content, Content rating) and answer it as a dating app with user-to-user video. The rating should come out as adults only.

Japan: check the online dating law before spending anything there.

As far as I know, a service that introduces strangers to each other for dating has to file a notification under Japan's online dating law (出会い系サイト規制法, the business is called インターネット異性紹介事業) and has to verify that every user is 18 or older. Google Ads asks dating advertisers in Japan for that notification before it will run their ads. If FaceMeet has not filed it, paid acquisition in Japan may not be possible at all yet, and half of the goal depends on it. Confirm this with a Japanese lawyer first.

Korea: the 19+ gate has to be a real one.

As far as I know, since December 2020 random chat apps in Korea are treated as harmful to minors unless sign-up is verified by real name or mobile phone and the app has working report functions. The listing already mentions reporting and blocking. Make sure sign-up uses mobile phone verification (휴대폰 본인인증), and say so in the listing: it is also a reason for adults to trust the app.

Google Ads treats dating as a restricted category.

The advertiser account has to be certified for dating ads, country by country, before an App campaign will serve. Apply now for Korea; approval takes days, not hours. Until it is approved, the only traffic is what the store listing brings by itself.

6. What 100 registrations a day takes

Registrations are installs multiplied by the share of installers who finish sign-up. For a 19+ app with phone verification that share is usually between a third and a half. Installs come from two places: people who find the listing by themselves (free, slow, and it grows with the fixes above) and paid campaigns. Do not commit a monthly budget to these numbers: run the seven-day test below and replace every figure in the tables with your own.

The first seven days

  1. Day 1. Redo the content rating. Publish the new Korean title, short and full description. Reorder the screenshots so a call comes first. All of this is free.
  2. Day 1. Apply for Google Ads dating certification for Korea. For Japan, ask a lawyer about the notification before anything else.
  3. Days 1 to 2. Add events for "registration completed" and "first call started" (Firebase) and import them into Google Ads. Without the registration event a campaign buys installs, not registrations.
  4. Days 2 to 8. Korea test: one App campaign optimised for the registration event, $40 to $60 a day, shown only between 19:00 and 01:00 Korean time.
  5. Day 8. Read three numbers: cost per install, install to registration, and the share of new members who start a call on their first day. Set the real budget from them.
  6. After that. Japan: add the Japanese listing and screenshot set, and start the same test once the legal question is answered.

Why evenings only, and one country first

You asked for people who register and then keep using the app. In a live video app that depends less on the listing than on how many people are online at the same moment.

  • Spread thin, nobody meets. 100 registrations spread evenly over 24 hours and two countries is about two new people an hour in each country. They open the app, find nobody to call, and do not come back.
  • Concentrated, they find each other. The same 50 in one country inside a six-hour evening window is about eight an hour, on top of the members you already have. Start with one country and the evening, and widen only when new members reliably get a call on day one.
  • Show that people are there. An "online now" count on the first screen and in the second screenshot does more for retention than any wording.

Free channels to run alongside

  • Search in the store. The new title and descriptions carry the terms people type. Expect the effect in two to four weeks, not days.
  • Reviews. Ask for a rating with the in-app review prompt after a call longer than three minutes. Never right after sign-up.
  • Invitations. Points for both sides when an invited friend finishes sign-up. Track it with the Play install referrer.
  • Short video. One 15-second screen recording a day on TikTok, Reels and Shorts, in Korean and in Japanese, with a tagged link to the listing.
  • Japan, if you use the Korea angle. Korean-language learners and K-culture communities are where "talk with people in Korea" is a selling point.

The first minutes inside the app

I have not used the app, so these are the places where apps of this kind usually lose most new members. Check each one.

  • Let people look before they sign up. Show who is online first. Ask for registration at the moment someone taps the call button.
  • Explain the phone check in one line. "Adults only, verified by phone" reads as safety. An unexplained verification step reads as a trap.
  • One real call for free. Give enough starting points for a first call, and show the price of points before the first purchase.
  • Ask for camera and microphone at the first call. Not at launch, when the person does not yet know why.
  • Japanese inside the app. If the sign-up flow is Korean only, Japanese installs will not turn into registrations whatever the listing says. Translate that flow first.
